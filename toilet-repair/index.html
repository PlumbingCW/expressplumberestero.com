A toilet that will not stop running or is rising toward the rim needs fast help. Call and we will get to the cause.

If the toilet is overflowing right now.

  1. 1Stop the fill.Turn the oval valve behind the toilet clockwise. If it is stuck, lift the tank lid and raise the float or flapper chain.
  2. 2Do not flush again.Another flush adds water to a bowl that is already full.
  3. 3Contain the water.Put towels down and lift rugs. Wear gloves, since the water may be dirty.
  4. 4Try a plunger once.A flange plunger can clear a simple clog. If water keeps rising, stop.
  5. 5Call the 24/7 line.Tell dispatch whether other drains are slow, since that points to a bigger line problem.

Common toilet problems and what they mean.

Symptoms and likely causes.
What you see.Likely cause.Usual fix.
Runs on and on.Worn flapper or fill valve.Replace the worn part inside the tank.
Weak or partial flush.Clogged rim jets or low tank water.Clean the jets and adjust the water level.
Water around the base.Failed wax ring or loose bolts.Pull the toilet, replace the seal and reset it.
Wobbles on the floor.Loose bolts or a damaged flange.Re-secure, or repair the flange.
Clogs again and again.Blockage deeper in the drain.Clear the trap and check the line.
Tank sweats or leaks.Cracked tank or failed gasket.Replace the gasket or the tank.

Many of these are small repairs, but they cost more when ignored. A running toilet wastes water around the clock. A leak at the base soaks the subfloor and the damage is out of sight. If a clog keeps returning, it may not be the toilet. See drain cleaning.

Repair or replace the toilet.

Signs a new toilet is the better choice.

  • A crack in the bowl or tank, even a hairline one.
  • Repeated repairs that do not hold.
  • Rust or staining in the porcelain that will not come clean.
  • A bowl that clogs constantly despite a clear drain.
  • An old, high-volume model when you want a lower flush volume.

If the porcelain is sound, the repair is usually the cheaper route. We tell you plainly which way makes sense, since we would rather fix a good toilet than sell a new one.

What affects the toilet repair price.

Factors that move the price.

Parts or full removal.
Swapping a flapper is quick. Pulling the toilet to reset the seal takes longer.
Hidden damage.
A long-term base leak may have softened the floor underneath.
Clog depth.
A clog in the bowl is simple. A clog in the drain behind it is a different job.
Replacement unit.
New toilets vary in style, height and flush type.
Shutoff and supply line condition.
Old valves and lines sometimes need replacing at the same time.

When it is more than the toilet.

If a tub gurgles when you flush or several drains run slowly, the problem is in the line, not the fixture. That calls for sewer line cleaning rather than another toilet repair. A leak at the base that keeps returning may involve the flange or the floor.
