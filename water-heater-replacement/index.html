When replacing beats repairing.

A water heater is a tank of hot water under constant pressure, and eventually the steel wears through. A leak from the tank body itself is the clearest sign. Repairs cannot patch a failed tank, so the answer is a new unit.

Many Estero homes were built between the late 1990s and the mid 2000s. A heater original to a house that age has had a long run. If yours is that old and has just failed, replacement usually makes more sense than a repair bill on a tired unit.

Signs the heater should be replaced.

  • Water pooling around the base of the tank, not just at a fitting.
  • Rust-colored hot water, or a tank that rumbles and pops when heating.
  • Repeated repairs in a short stretch of time.
  • A unit that is well past a decade old and has stopped heating.
  • Hot water that runs out far faster than it used to.

Tank, tankless or heat pump: which fits your house.

Each type suits a different household. The table below is a starting point, and we confirm on site what your plumbing, venting and power supply allow.

Water heater types compared.
Type.Best for.Things to know.
Standard tank, gas or electric.Most homes, fastest swap.Lowest upfront cost. Keeps a tank of hot water ready, so the heater cycles even when nobody is using it.
Tankless.Smaller households and endless hot water.Heats on demand. Needs the right gas line or electrical capacity and venting. See tankless service.
Heat pump tank.Homes with space and a long view on running cost.Needs clearance and airflow around the unit. Uses less electricity than a standard electric tank.

What affects the cost.

Factors that move the price.

Tank or tankless.
Tankless units and heat pump tanks cost more than a standard tank. Their setup is also more involved.
Capacity.
A larger family needs a larger tank. Sizing the unit to the house avoids cold showers.
Gas, electric or both.
Swapping like for like is the simplest. Changing fuel type adds work.
Venting and code updates.
Older installs sometimes need venting or a pan and drain brought up to current rules.
Access.
A cramped closet or attic takes longer than a garage.

Getting ready before the plumber arrives.

Shut the water off.

Close the cold supply valve at the heater, or the main valve if you cannot find it.

Kill the power or gas supply.

Turn off the breaker or the gas valve to the heater only. Leave the rest of the house on.

Clear the path.

Move boxes and stored items so the plumber can reach the unit and carry the old one out.

Note what you know.

Brand, age and any model label help us bring the right parts.
