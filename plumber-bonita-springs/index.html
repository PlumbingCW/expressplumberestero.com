Homes that sit empty part of the year.

The 2020 census counted 38,577 housing units in Bonita Springs and tagged 35.5 percent of them vacant. That count includes seasonal homes, so many of those units are simply closed up for the summer. A closed house with the water on is where small leaks grow.

If you own a seasonal home here, a failed supply line or a running toilet can waste water for weeks before anyone sees it. Shut the main when you leave, and ask a neighbor or manager to check inside. Our leak detection visit helps when the bill jumps and nothing looks wet.

Leaving for the season.

Before a long absence, turn off the main valve, switch the water heater to vacation or off, and photograph the meter. A reading on your return that differs from the photo means water moved while you were gone.

Storm seasons and the plumbing they leave behind.

Hurricane Irma's eye passed over Bonita Springs on September 9 and 10, 2017. Charley in 2004 and Wilma in 2005 also hit close by. Storms like these shake loose pipe joints, flood water heater closets and push debris into drains.

After any major storm, check for low pressure, discolored water and slow drains before you assume the house came through fine. Water heaters that sat in floodwater should be inspected before you relight them. Gas and electric parts do not mix with wet conditions.

Permits and licensing in the city.

Water heater swaps, repipes and sewer repairs may need a permit. Rules vary, so ask the City of Bonita Springs building department before work begins. Do not rely on us or anyone else to guess it over the phone.

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition took effect on December 31, 2023, and newer permits follow it. Verify any plumber at myfloridalicense.com by searching the name or licence number and confirming the status is current and active.
