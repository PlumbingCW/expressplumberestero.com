What Coconut Point is.

Coconut Point is an outdoor shopping and dining center in Estero, east of US-41, and the name also covers surrounding homes. A 2016 Estero profile calls it the town's crown jewel, with shops, restaurants and condominiums. A 2013 planning study described a mixed-use development of about 1,200 homes plus retail and a medical complex.

That means we treat Coconut Point as a district, not a neighborhood with one housing type. The sections below cover businesses first, then residents.

Shops, restaurants and tenant spaces.

Commercial spaces have their own plumbing questions. A restaurant kitchen has floor drains, hand sinks, dishwashers and often a grease interceptor. A retail shop may have only restrooms and a mop sink. A medical office has its own fixtures.

In a leased space, the lease decides what the tenant fixes and what the landlord fixes. Check it before you pay for a repair, and tell dispatch who can approve the work.

Apartments and homes around the center.

A local development summary lists The Reserve at Coconut Point, 180 apartments at the southeast corner of Williams Road and Via Coconut Point, as complete. It also lists a final phase of Genova with 40 villas and townhomes, and Coconut Landing, 25 single-family homes off Coconut Road.

A 2013 planning study counted 540 homes in the Rapallo North Village of Coconut Point. Rapallo and the newer communities are different ages, so a plumbing question for one may not suit another.

Williams Road runs through this district.

The Village of Estero says Lee County Utilities serves properties north of Williams Road and Bonita Springs Utilities serves properties south of it. Coconut Point lies along Williams Road, so two buildings a short walk apart may have different water providers.

Check each bill to see which utility serves your address. That matters when a problem sits on the utility's side of the meter.

Sources: Village of Estero utilities page, Esterotoday development summary, Estero Community Market Assessment (2013), a 2016 Estero profile hosted on the Village website.

Leaks and drains in a busy district.

A restroom that overflows during a busy weekend costs more than a plumber's visit. If a toilet or floor drain starts to back up, close the nearest valve and call. A camera look decides whether you need drain cleaning or a repair.

For a hidden leak behind a finished wall, leak detection finds the source before walls are opened. Water that has no obvious source often has one under the floor.

Commercial permits depend on the project. Before work that changes pipes, fixtures or a water heater, check with the Village's Community Development department instead of assuming an exemption.
