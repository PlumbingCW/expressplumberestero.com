Why Drains Clog in Estero Homes: The Usual Causes and What They Look Like

A slow drain is a warning. Where it happens tells you a lot about why.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

The usual suspects, in short.

Most clogs come from a handful of things: grease and food in kitchen lines, hair and soap in bathroom lines, wipes and paper products in toilets, and tree roots or damaged pipe in buried drains. The cause usually shows in where the problem appears.

One slow sink usually means a local clog in that fixture's trap or branch line. Several fixtures slowing at once, or gurgling when another fixture drains, usually means a blockage further down the main line.

Kitchen clogs: grease and what goes down the sink.

Fat and oil look liquid when hot, then cool and cling to the inside of the pipe. Over months, food bits stick to that film and the opening narrows. Many callers describe a kitchen sink that clears for a week and then slows again, which is typical of a grease lining.

Coffee grounds, rice, pasta and fibrous scraps pack into drain lines even with a disposal. A disposal grinds food, but it does not stop the grease from coating the pipe downstream.

Bathroom clogs: hair, soap and wipes.

Hair knots with soap scum and forms a mat near the top of a tub or shower drain. Pulling it out with a drain tool often fixes a slow tub. A plunger or a hand auger is safe for this level of problem.

Toilets clog from too much paper or from wipes labeled flushable. Those wipes do not break down the way toilet paper does. They gather in bends and snag other waste, which is a common reason a toilet clog returns.

Never mix drain chemicals.

Do not combine drain cleaners, and do not plunge after pouring one in. Mixed chemicals can release dangerous fumes or splash caustic liquid. If a chemical cleaner has already failed, tell the plumber what you used so they can work safely.

Main line clogs: roots, belly and buildup.

Roots grow toward moisture and work into joints, then thicken until they catch debris. A sag or belly in the pipe collects waste and creates a repeat clog. Old, cracked pipe lets soil in.

Florida homes sit on sandy soils, and shifting ground can open joints over the years. We cannot tell the cause from the phone. A camera inspection shows whether the line holds roots, a crack, a belly or just buildup. See sewer camera inspection for how that works.

Signs the problem is in the main line.

  • Several drains slow or back up at the same time.
  • Gurgling from toilets or tubs when you run the washer or a sink.
  • Sewage smell in the yard or near a floor drain.
  • Water pooling around a cleanout or in the lowest tub.
  • Clogs that clear and return within days or weeks.

Local angle: homes built in the 1990s and 2000s.

Many Estero homes date from the late 1990s through the mid 2000s, so drain lines are often twenty to thirty-five years old. We do not assume a pipe material for a given community. Older lines simply have had more years to collect buildup, shift or let roots in.

Sewer service providers differ by address, and we have not verified which provider covers which area. Ask your utility who maintains the line past your property. Anything on your side of the property line is generally the homeowner's responsibility. Your bill or the utility can confirm the boundary.

Fixing it, and keeping it from coming back.

For a stubborn buildup, hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the inside of the pipe instead of just poking a hole through the blockage. A camera look afterward shows whether the pipe is sound. If it is damaged, sewer line repair may be the lasting fix.

At home, keep grease out of the sink, use drain screens, and flush only toilet paper. We serve the Village of Estero and Bonita Springs around the clock.

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition governs how home drains are vented and graded. A drain that keeps clogging after a clean clear may have a design or condition problem, and not only debris.

Houses built decades ago, as in San Carlos Park, have had longer for buildup to collect in the drain lines, so a repeat clog there is worth a camera look.

Why the code year matters for drains.

Home drains are vented and graded to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing. A drain that keeps slowing after a proper clearing may point to a slope or vent fault from the original install, and no cleaner will fix that.

Permits applied for after December 31, 2023 follow the 8th Edition, so ask the Village before any drain rework.
