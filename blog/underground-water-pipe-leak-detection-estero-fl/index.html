How Plumbers Find a Leak in a Buried Water Line

A buried pipe leaks out of sight. Finding it means narrowing the search step by step before anyone digs.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

What detection can and cannot do.

Underground water pipe leak detection means confirming that a buried line is losing water and then narrowing down where. No single tool does both jobs perfectly. A plumber combines a meter test, a pressure test and listening gear, then marks the most likely spot.

The honest limit is that these tools point to a location. They do not show a picture of the crack. Digging is the final proof, and good detection keeps that dig small.

Signs a buried line may be leaking.

What callers notice first.

  • The water meter keeps moving after every fixture inside and outside is off.
  • A soft, soggy or unusually green strip of yard with no recent rain.
  • Water bubbling up from the ground or pooling near the meter box.
  • Low water pressure across the whole house, not at one tap.
  • A water bill that climbs month after month without a change in use.
  • A hissing sound near an outside wall or the meter.

Any one of these can have another cause, such as a running toilet or an irrigation valve stuck open. The tests below help separate those from a true buried leak.

Step one: rule out the easy causes.

  1. 1Check the meter.Turn off every fixture, appliance and the irrigation system. Watch the meter's small leak indicator or low-flow dial for a few minutes. If it moves, water is going somewhere.
  2. 2Test the toilets.Add a few drops of food coloring to each tank. If color shows in the bowl without a flush, that toilet is leaking.
  3. 3Shut the home off at the main.Close the main valve at the house and watch the meter again. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and your shutoff valve, which points to the buried service line.
  4. 4Isolate the irrigation.Irrigation lines are buried too, and a stuck zone valve is a common false alarm.
  5. 5Call a plumber with the results.Tell dispatch what you saw. It saves time on site.

The tools a plumber uses to pin it down.

Buried leak methods and their limits.
Method.What it does.Limit.
Meter and shutoff test.Confirms that water is leaving the system and narrows it to a section.Does not give a location.
Pressure test.Holds a section under pressure to see whether it drops.Needs the line isolated. Shows a loss, not a place.
Acoustic listening.Ground microphones and contact listeners pick up the sound of escaping water.Works best on pressurized lines. Traffic, irrigation and soft soil can hide the sound.
Pipe locator.Traces the route of the pipe so listening starts on the right path.Finds the line, not the leak.
Tracer gas.A harmless gas mix is pushed into an empty line and sensed at the surface.Takes more setup. Dense paving or deep pipe can weaken the signal.

Why a camera is the wrong tool here.

A sewer camera shows the inside of a drain. A water supply line is under pressure and full of water, so a camera does not apply. If a wet patch could be a drain problem, that is a separate job and a different tool. See sewer camera inspection.

Who owns the buried pipe in Estero.

Utility side or house side.

Estero water comes from two utilities. According to a Village of Estero water workshop document, Bonita Springs Utilities serves south of Williams Road and Lee County Utilities serves north of it. Boundaries can change, so check your bill.

Where the utility's responsibility ends and yours begins depends on the utility. Usually that is near the meter, but ask your utility before assuming. If the leak is before the meter, it may be theirs to fix. If it is after, it is yours.

Source: Village of Estero water workshop document, 2018.

That tie-back matters for decisions. If your meter spins but the leak sits on the utility's side, you want to know before paying a plumber to dig. A plumber's meter test helps show which side the water is leaving from.

What to do while you wait for help.

  • Shut the main valve if water is surfacing or the leak is large.
  • Keep people and pets away from soft ground near a pooling leak, since the soil can shift.
  • Never dig near the area before the utilities are marked. Call 811, the national call-before-you-dig line, so buried gas and electric lines are located first.
  • Photograph the wet area and the meter reading with the time and date.
  • Do not run irrigation until you know which line is leaking.

Can you fix it without digging up the yard?

Sometimes. Once the leak is located, a spot repair needs only a small hole around that point. A short run of damaged pipe can be cut out and replaced. A badly worn line may need a longer replacement, and a plumber explains the choice after a look.

If one leak shows up, ask whether the rest of the line is in similar shape. A second leak a few months later costs more than a joint plan. Our burst pipe repair page covers fast fixes. If the whole line is tired, repiping is the longer answer.

When to call.

Call the same day if you see water surfacing, hear hissing at the meter or lose pressure suddenly. Call within a few days if the bill keeps rising or the yard stays wet after dry weather. A small buried leak wastes water every hour and can wash out the soil under pavers and slabs.
