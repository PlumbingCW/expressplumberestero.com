Repiping a House on a Concrete Slab: What the Job Involves

A slab does not rule out a repipe. It changes the route the new pipes take.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

Yes, a slab house can be repiped.

A house on a concrete slab can be repiped, and most are done without breaking up the floor. The usual method runs new water lines through the attic or the walls and abandons the old lines under the concrete. The new system reaches every fixture, while the slab stays intact.

The drain lines are a separate question. Those also run under the slab, and replacing them is a bigger project. A camera inspection tells you whether they need work at all.

Three ways to handle the pipes.

Repipe approaches for a slab house.
Approach.How it works.Trade-off.
Reroute through attic or walls.New supply lines run overhead or inside walls. The old slab lines are capped.Avoids breaking the floor. Opens drywall at some points.
Replace under the slab.The slab is cut, trenches are dug and new pipe is laid.Keeps the original layout. Heavy, messy and slow.
Spot repair.One failed section is fixed or bypassed.Cheapest in the short run. Does not stop the next failure.

Most homeowners with a failing slab system choose the reroute. It is faster than cutting concrete and keeps the new pipes where a plumber can reach them later.

Signs a repipe is worth considering.

When repiping makes more sense than another patch.

  • You have had more than one leak in the same system.
  • Water comes out discolored, brown or with a metallic taste.
  • Pressure is low at several fixtures, not just one.
  • The pipes are a type with a known history of failure in your area. Ask a plumber what is behind your walls.
  • A leak was found under the slab and more pinholes are likely.
  • The house is old enough that the original lines are near the end of their life.

If you are not sure whether one leak or many, start with leak detection. Knowing where the water is going keeps you from replacing a whole system for a single failure.

What the job looks like.

  1. 1Inspect and plan.The plumber maps fixtures, the water heater, the main and the route the new pipe will take.
  2. 2Price and schedule.You get a clear price before work starts, along with a plan for the days the water will be off.
  3. 3Protect the house.Floors, furniture and fixtures get covered. Access points in drywall are marked.
  4. 4Run the new lines.New pipe goes in through the attic, walls or the chosen route and connects to each fixture.
  5. 5Test under pressure.The system is filled and pressure tested to check every joint before walls are closed.
  6. 6Patch and clean up.Openings in drywall are patched and the old shutoffs and connections are capped or removed.

Plan for days without full water.

Most repipes cut the water off for part of a day, and some leave you with limited service while sections are finished. Tell your household and plan for showers, laundry and cooking.

Renters and tenants also need notice. If you manage a property, coordinate access with whoever lives there before the day starts.

Estero homes and what the age means.

Age of the house is the first clue.

Many Estero homes were built between the late 1990s and the mid 2000s, and the older neighborhoods nearby run older still. Plumbing at that age is not automatically failing, but it is the time owners start to see leaks. We cannot tell the pipe material from the phone, so a plumber should look at yours.

The area south of Williams Road gets water from Bonita Springs Utilities, and the area north gets it from Lee County Utilities, according to a Village of Estero water workshop document. Ask your utility about the water chemistry, since it can affect how pipes and fixtures age. The sources we checked did not give a hardness figure.

Source: Village of Estero water workshop document, 2018.

Materials and code.

Common modern choices for water supply lines are PEX, copper and CPVC. Each has trade-offs in cost, durability and installation. A plumber should explain which suits your house and why.

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition took effect on December 31, 2023, and permits applied for after that date follow it. Repiping is often permitted work, but rules vary, so ask the Village of Estero Community Development department. San Carlos Park is unincorporated, so San Carlos Park homeowners should ask Lee County.

Questions to ask before you hire.

  • Which pipe material do you recommend for my house, and why that one?
  • Where will the new lines run, and which walls or ceilings will you open?
  • Who pulls the permit, and who schedules the inspection?
  • What happens to the old lines under the slab, capped or left in place?
  • How long will the water be off, and on which days?
  • Is drywall patching included, and does painting fall to me?

A good plumber answers these without hesitation and puts the scope in writing before work starts. If an answer is vague, keep asking until it is not.

Honest limits and DIY.

A whole-house repipe is not a weekend DIY project. It involves pressure testing, shutting down the main supply, permit handling and careful work around the water heater. Mistakes show up as leaks inside finished walls.

You can help by clearing access, noting where leaks have happened and checking your plumber's licence at myfloridalicense.com. If a slab leak is the trigger, our slab leak repair page explains when a spot fix is enough.

Owners in Bonita Springs who spend part of the year away can plan repiping around their absence, since the water has to be off for a stretch.
