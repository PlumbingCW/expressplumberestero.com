Toilet Still Clogged After Plunging? What to Try and When to Call

A plunger that does nothing usually means the blockage is firm, far down the line or part of a bigger problem.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

Why the plunger fails and what to try next.

A plunger fails when the clog is too firm to push through, too far down the trap or not in the toilet at all. A solid object like a toy or a wad of wipes will not budge with pressure. A blockage past the toilet, in the branch or main line, will not clear from the bowl.

Before you try anything else, stop flushing. Each flush adds water and can push it over the rim. Then work through the steps below, in order.

  1. 1Stop the water.Lift the tank lid and close the flapper, or turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise. That prevents the tank refilling the bowl.
  2. 2Check the plunger.A flat cup plunger works on sinks. A toilet needs a flange plunger with a rubber flap that fits into the drain opening.
  3. 3Seal and push gently.Cover the opening completely, push slowly to remove air, then pull sharply. Repeat for a minute or two.
  4. 4Try hot, not boiling, water and dish soap.Pour a squirt of dish soap into the bowl, add warm tap water and wait fifteen minutes. Boiling water can crack porcelain.
  5. 5Use a closet auger.A short toilet auger reaches past the bend. Feed it in, crank gently and pull back any blockage.
  6. 6Test with a small flush.Pour a bucket of water into the bowl instead of flushing. If it drains, the clog is gone.

What not to do.

Skip the chemical drain cleaners.

Never pour drain cleaner into a clogged toilet. The liquid sits in the bowl, can splash back onto skin and eyes and can damage plumbing. Mixing two products can release dangerous fumes.

If a chemical has already gone in, tell the plumber. It changes how we handle the clog, and we wear protection.

  • Do not keep flushing hoping the clog gives way.
  • Do not use a wire coat hanger. It scratches the porcelain and can lodge in the trap.
  • Do not mix bleach with other cleaners.
  • Do not force a snake that meets hard resistance. It can crack the toilet or push the object deeper.
  • Do not ignore gurgling in other drains. It points to a deeper problem.

If water appears in a shower or tub when you flush, treat it as a main line problem. Sewer line cleaning clears the line, and a camera inspection can show why it blocked.

Common reasons toilets clog.

Things that block a toilet.

  • Too much toilet paper in a single flush.
  • Wipes, even those labeled flushable.
  • Feminine products, cotton swabs and dental floss.
  • Toys or small objects dropped in by children.
  • A low-flow toilet that struggles with heavy loads.
  • A partial clog that builds up slowly over weeks.
  • Tree roots or a belly in the sewer line, which show up as repeated clogs.

When to call a plumber.

Call if the plunger and auger both fail, if more than one fixture backs up or if sewage appears anywhere in the house. Call if the toilet clogs again soon after clearing, since that means the cause is still there.

A licensed plumber can clear the toilet, pull it if needed to retrieve an object and check the line. Our drain cleaning crew handles the branch lines, and a toilet that runs or leaks at the base is a separate toilet repair job. If the main line is involved, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall rather than punching a hole through the blockage.

Safety first. If sewage has spilled, keep children and pets away, avoid skin contact and ventilate the area. Wash your hands well afterward. Electrical items on a wet floor are a shock risk, so keep cords away from the water.
