How to Fix a Dripping Bathtub Faucet and Know When to Stop

A dripping tub spout wastes water around the clock. Here is how to find the cause and decide who should fix it.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

The short answer on a dripping tub faucet.

Most dripping bathtub faucets come from a worn cartridge, a worn washer or a failed seal inside the valve. The handle feels loose or hard to turn, and water keeps trickling after you shut it off. Fixing it means replacing the worn part, and on many tubs that is a one-visit job.

The catch is access. The valve sits inside the wall, so you have to remove the handle and trim first. If the shutoff does not stop the water or the wall feels damp, stop and call a plumber.

Where the water is coming from tells you the cause.

Tub and shower drip locations and likely causes.
Where it drips.Likely cause.DIY or plumber.
Tub spout, handle is off.Worn cartridge, washer or seat inside the valve.DIY possible if the parts are easy to identify.
Showerhead after shutoff.Same valve wear, or a diverter that does not seal.Either, depending on access.
Around the handle.Loose packing nut or a cracked stem seal.DIY for a simple tighten, plumber if it keeps leaking.
Where the spout meets the wall.Failed caulk or a loose spout connection.Often DIY.
Wall or ceiling below.Leak inside the wall or at the valve body.Plumber, right away.

Notice that the last row is the one that matters most. A drip you can see is a nuisance. A drip behind tile can soak drywall and framing for weeks before you notice a stain.

Steps to try before you call.

  1. 1Shut off the water.Look for a stop valve behind an access panel, or close the main shutoff. Open the tub tap to confirm the flow has stopped.
  2. 2Plug the drain.A small screw or washer lost down the tub drain is a common and annoying mistake. Cover the opening with a rag.
  3. 3Remove the handle and trim.Pry off the cap, back out the screw and slide the handle off. Take a photo before you move anything else.
  4. 4Pull the cartridge or stem.Many valves use a retaining clip or nut. Note which way the cartridge faces before it comes out.
  5. 5Match the part, not the guess.Bring the old cartridge to a supply store and match it exactly. Brands and generations differ, and a close match often fails.
  6. 6Reassemble and test.Turn the water back on slowly. Check the spout, the showerhead and the wall for any moisture.

Mineral buildup can mimic a bad part.

If the valve looks crusty with white or tan deposits, the seals may be worn by scale. Cleaning the cartridge sometimes helps, but a cartridge that has pitted or scored seats needs replacing.

We cannot tell from the phone whether a part is cleanable or finished. A hands-on look settles it.

Estero homes and the age of the valve.

Why age matters on a tub valve.

Many Estero homes were built between the late 1990s and the mid 2000s. A tub valve that old has been opened and closed thousands of times, and its internal seals wear whether or not you notice a drip. If yours is original to the house, a replacement cartridge or a full valve swap may be the longer-lasting fix.

Ask your utility how your supply behaves, because scale on a valve is worth knowing about before you buy parts. The sources we checked did not give a hardness figure.

When to stop and call a plumber.

Call a plumber if you see any of these.

  • The water does not stop when you close the stop valve or main.
  • The wall, the ceiling below or the tile grout feels damp or looks stained.
  • The handle, stem or cartridge will not come out without force.
  • The valve is a single-lever or pressure-balanced type and you cannot identify the brand.
  • You hear water running inside the wall with every tap closed.
  • Hot water comes out when you turn the tap to cold, or the temperature swings.

If the drip has been going a while and the wall behind the tub is wet, that moves from a faucet job to a leak job. Our crew can pinpoint where the water is traveling, and leak detection methods find the source without opening more wall than needed.

A dripping fixture can also mean the line feeding it is under pressure stress. When several faucets in the house drip or hammer, a plumber should check the supply side as well.

What drives the cost of a tub faucet repair.

Factors that move the price of a tub faucet fix.

Cartridge or full valve.
Swapping a cartridge is a smaller job than replacing the valve body behind the wall.
Access to the valve.
A back-to-back access panel is quick. Tile and no panel mean cutting an opening and patching it.
Fixture brand and age.
Common brands have parts on the shelf. Older or discontinued valves may need an adapter or a new valve.
Water damage found.
A wet wall adds drying, repair and sometimes a leak search to the visit.
Trim and finish.
New handle and trim kits cost more than a cartridge alone, and some tubs look better with the matching set.

Fix, replace or upgrade.

If only the cartridge is worn, replace it and keep the valve. If the body is corroded, cracked or leaking at the connections, the valve itself should come out. Plan on a full replacement when the trim is also dated and you are tired of fighting it.

Our faucet repair crew can talk through those options on site. If you also want a new fixture set in a remodel, plumbing installation covers new valves and fixtures.

Licensed plumbers do this work in Florida, and you can check anyone's licence status at myfloridalicense.com before you hire. Wall work and valve replacement can fall under permit rules, and rules vary, so ask the Village of Estero Community Development department if you are not sure.

Quick safety notes.

  • Always shut the water off before you take the handle off.
  • Do not use pliers on chrome trim without a cloth, since they scar the finish.
  • If the tub is wet, keep electrical items away. Never plug in a hair dryer or a radio near water.
  • Do not force a stuck cartridge. A cracked valve body turns a small repair into a wall repair.
  • If water is dripping near a light fixture or fan below, turn that circuit off and call.

If the tub faucet is dripping in Bonita Springs or San Carlos Park, closing the tub shutoff first limits the waste while you wait.
