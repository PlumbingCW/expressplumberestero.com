Warning Signs of a Slab Leak in an Estero Home

Many Florida homes sit on a concrete slab, so a hidden leak below the floor is a real possibility. Here is how to spot one early.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

The signs in plain terms.

A slab leak is a break or pinhole in a water line that runs under or inside the concrete floor of your home. The clearest warning signs are a water meter that keeps moving with everything off, a warm patch on the floor, the sound of running water in a quiet house and a water bill that climbs without explanation.

None of these proves a slab leak on its own. Together, they are strong evidence, and a plumber with the right equipment can confirm it without breaking up your floor.

Slab leak warning signs to watch for.

  • The water meter dial or leak indicator keeps moving when every fixture is off.
  • A warm or hot spot on the tile or carpet, often where a hot line runs under the floor.
  • The sound of rushing or hissing water when the house is quiet.
  • A water bill that rises for no reason you can name.
  • Damp carpet, lifted flooring or a musty smell with no visible source.
  • Cracks in grout or tile that appear along a line.
  • Low water pressure across the house, or hot water that arrives slowly.
  • Water pooling outside near the foundation or along the slab edge.

The ten-minute meter test.

This check tells you whether water is leaving the house through a line you cannot see. It does not say where, and it cannot separate a slab leak from a leak in the yard.

  1. 1Stop all water use.Turn off taps, dishwashers, washers and ice makers. Make sure nobody flushes a toilet.
  2. 2Find the meter.It is usually in a box near the street. Lift the lid carefully and watch for insects.
  3. 3Read the leak indicator.Many meters have a small triangle or dial that spins when any water moves. Note the position.
  4. 4Wait and look again.Wait 15 to 30 minutes with no water used. If the dial moved or the numbers rose, water is going somewhere.
  5. 5Rule out the easy causes.Listen for a running toilet and check for dripping outdoor spigots or irrigation. If you find none, call.

A running toilet can fool the test.

A toilet that leaks silently from tank to bowl can move the meter all day. Add a few drops of food coloring to each tank and wait. If the color shows in the bowl without flushing, you have found a different leak.

The test points toward a problem. It cannot tell us the cause, which is why a camera or listening device matters.

Why slab homes in Estero are worth watching.

Home age and your utility.

Many Estero homes were built between the late 1990s and the mid 2000s, so a lot of the plumbing is now two to three decades old. Pipe wear and joint failure are more likely at that age, and water lines in a slab can be hard to reach. That is a reason to act on early signs rather than wait for a wet floor.

Water comes from Lee County Utilities north of Williams Road and Bonita Springs Utilities south of it, according to a Village of Estero water workshop document. Your bill shows which one serves you. A sudden jump in usage is worth asking the utility about, since they can sometimes flag unusual consumption.

What to do first.

Safe first moves.

Shut off the main.

Close the main shutoff valve at the house. That stops the leak if the line is under pressure.

Watch for electrical risks.

If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, stay away and turn off power only if you can reach the breaker dry.

Smell for gas.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside. Do not use switches or phones inside.

Document it.

Take photos of wet spots, the meter and any damage. Your insurer may ask for them.

How a plumber confirms a slab leak.

We start with the pressure and flow clues you give us, then use listening equipment, thermal imaging or a pressure test to narrow it down. The goal is a precise spot, so the repair opens as little floor as possible. Leak detection is the step that tells you what you are dealing with.

If the line is confirmed, the options are usually a spot repair, rerouting the line through the walls or ceiling or replacing the whole run. We lay out the choices and the reasons before any work starts. The details are on our slab leak repair page.

Things you should not do.

  • Do not drill or saw into the slab to look for the pipe.
  • Do not assume a high bill is only usage. Check the meter first.
  • Do not pour drain chemicals into any line hoping to clear a leak.
  • Do not ignore a warm floor spot. Hot water lines under a slab leak quietly and constantly.
  • Do not wait if the main shutoff will not close. That is an emergency.
