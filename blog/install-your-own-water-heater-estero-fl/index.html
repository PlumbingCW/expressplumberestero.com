Should You Swap Your Own Water Heater in Estero or Hire It Out?

Swapping a water heater looks like a weekend job. Gas, electric and hot water under pressure make it riskier than it appears.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

Can a homeowner do it? The direct answer.

Some homeowners do swap an electric tank heater themselves, and some are comfortable with the basics. A gas unit is a different matter, because a bad gas connection or vent can leak fuel or carbon monoxide into the house. We recommend a licensed plumber for gas units and for any change in fuel, location or venting.

Before you buy anything, ask the Village of Estero Community Development department what applies to your address. Permit rules vary, and Florida law and local rules decide who may do the work, not us.

What the job actually involves.

  1. 1Shut off supply and energy.Close the cold water valve, then turn off the breaker or the gas valve to the heater.
  2. 2Drain the old tank.Attach a hose to the drain valve and open a hot tap upstairs so it can empty. A full tank is heavy and hot.
  3. 3Disconnect and remove.Unhook the water lines, the gas or electrical connection, the vent and the relief valve pipe. Carry the old tank out.
  4. 4Set the new unit.Place it level on a pan or stand as required, with the right clearances around it.
  5. 5Reconnect lines and fittings.Use new flex connectors, a proper relief valve and drain line, and tight, tested joints.
  6. 6Fill, test and light or power up.Fill the tank, bleed the air, check every joint for leaks, then restore power or gas and check operation.

Each step has places where a small mistake becomes a flood or a safety problem. The relief valve and its drain line, for example, protect against pressure and heat building up in the tank. Skipping or capping them is dangerous.

Where do-it-yourself swaps go wrong.

Common first-time mistakes.

  • Reusing old flexible connectors or shutoff valves that were already corroded.
  • Leaving air in the system, which makes the unit sputter or run on a dry element.
  • Energizing an electric heater before the tank is full, which burns out the elements.
  • Poor venting on a gas unit, which can pull exhaust back into the home.
  • Skipping the relief valve drain line, or running it somewhere unsafe.
  • Missing the permit, then finding out when selling or filing an insurance claim.

The gas and carbon monoxide line.

If you ever smell gas, leave the house immediately and call the gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches or use a phone indoors. Install a carbon monoxide detector near any gas appliance, and never ignore its alarm.

Permits, code and the Village of Estero.

The Village of Estero Community Development department takes building and trade permit applications, and the Village website has a Trade Permit application. Whether swapping a water heater needs one at your address is a question for them, since rules vary.

Permits applied for after December 31, 2023 follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition. If you live in an unincorporated area such as San Carlos Park, ask Lee County instead. An unpermitted install can raise questions with an insurer or a buyer later, so ask first and keep the paperwork.

What you save and what you risk.

What a DIY swap really involves cost-wise.

The unit itself.
You pay for the heater either way, and a plumber can often source one that fits your closet.
Parts people forget.
Connectors, valves, a drain pan, venting pieces and a proper relief valve add up.
Tools and hauling.
Hoses, wrenches, a dolly and a way to dispose of the old tank.
Mistake risk.
One bad joint can soak a wall or floor, and water damage costs more than the labor you saved.
Permit and inspection.
If a permit applies, it applies whether you or a plumber does the work.

A clear price from a licensed plumber before work starts is the fair comparison, not parts alone.

When to stop and call right now.

Stop the DIY if.

  • You smell gas or see a pilot or burner flame that looks wrong.
  • The tank is leaking and you cannot shut off water at the unit.
  • The vent pipe is corroded, loose or does not slope the way the old one did.
  • Water is dripping near the electrical connection or the panel.
  • You are unsure of the voltage, the wire size or the breaker rating.

Standing water or no hot water now? Call and we will plan the swap with you.
