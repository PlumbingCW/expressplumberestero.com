Thermal Imaging for Leaks: What the Camera Really Sees

Thermal cameras make for striking photos. The useful question is what they can prove about a leak, and what they cannot.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

What an infrared camera does and does not show.

An infrared camera, also called a thermal imager, shows surface temperature as a color picture. It does not see water, and it does not see through walls. It shows places where a surface is warmer or cooler than the area around it.

A leak can cause that kind of difference. Wet material often runs cooler than dry material because water evaporates. A hot water line leaking into a slab can warm one patch of floor. The camera spots the temperature pattern, and a person has to work out whether water caused it.

A thermal image is a clue, not proof.

Many things change surface temperature, including air conditioning ducts, a sun-warmed wall, missing insulation and a cold drink glass. A reputable plumber treats the image as a reason to test, then confirms with a moisture meter, a pressure test or listening gear.

Where thermal imaging helps.

Good uses for an infrared camera.

  • Scanning a ceiling or wall to find where a stain is widest before cutting a small access hole.
  • Tracing a hot water line leak under a floor, where the warm patch can show on the surface.
  • Checking a wall around a shower, tub or washer box for cool, wet-looking zones.
  • Finding cool spots along an outside wall that suggest moisture got in.
  • Narrowing a search so listening gear starts in the right room.

In each case, the camera shrinks the search area. It does not replace the next step.

Southwest Florida adds its own wrinkle. Warm, humid air and cold air conditioning ducts produce plenty of cool patches on ceilings and walls. A thermal image taken in an A/C-cooled room will show many of them. A plumber who knows the area reads those patterns with care.

Slab leaks and the warm floor question.

Many Estero homes are built on a concrete slab, with supply lines running under or in it. A hot water line that leaks can warm a spot on the floor, and bare feet often notice it before any camera does. Thermal imaging can show that patch clearly on tile or laminate.

It is still not the end of the investigation. A warm spot could also come from a hot water line close to the surface that is not leaking. Our page on slab leak repair explains the full process, including how the leak is confirmed.

What to do while you wait.

Check the meter.

Turn everything off and see whether the leak indicator moves.

Shut off the water if it is active.

Close the main valve if water is spreading or ceiling drywall is sagging.

Protect the electrics.

If a ceiling is dripping near lights, fans or outlets, shut off the breaker for that area and keep away from the wet zone.

Move belongings.

Lift rugs and furniture so wet floors can dry and the plumber can reach the area.

If a ceiling bulges with water, stay out from under it and keep people away. The weight can bring the drywall down without warning, so call right away.
