Is a Sewer Camera Look Worth It for Your Estero Home?

A sewer camera turns a guess into a picture. It also has limits, and knowing them keeps you from paying for the wrong thing.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

The short answer.

A sewer camera inspection is worth it when you have repeat clogs, a slow main drain, sewage smells, or a home purchase with an older line. It is less useful on a house with no symptoms and a recently replaced line.

The camera is a small waterproof lens on a flexible cable. It travels down the drain and shows the pipe from the inside. You see cracks, roots, sags, offsets and buildup, and the plumber can mark where they sit.

What the camera shows.

Problems a sewer camera can reveal.

  • Tree or shrub roots that entered through a joint or crack.
  • A cracked, broken or collapsed section of pipe.
  • A sag, sometimes called a belly, where water and waste pool.
  • Joints that have shifted out of line.
  • Grease, scale or debris coating the pipe wall.
  • A foreign object that is stuck in the line.

Most lines show some wear. The question is whether what you see explains your symptom or threatens the house. A plumber looks at the picture with you and explains which findings matter.

What the camera cannot tell you.

Honest limits of the picture.

A camera shows what is in front of the lens. It does not measure how much longer a pipe will last, and it cannot see through a full blockage. If the line is packed solid, it may need cleaning before the camera can pass.

It also shows only the section it travels through. A bend, a standing pool of water or a wrong-size fitting can stop it, and the plumber should tell you what was not seen.

The camera also does not locate the pipe on the surface. A locator at the lens position marks the ground above it, which is a second tool and a second step. Ask about it if you are planning a dig.

How an inspection goes, step by step.

  1. 1Find the access point.The plumber uses a clean-out outside the house if there is one. If not, a toilet may need to be pulled, or a roof vent may serve as the entry.
  2. 2Clean if needed.A heavily coated or blocked line is cleared first, because the lens cannot see through sludge. See sewer line cleaning.
  3. 3Run the camera.The cable feeds slowly down the line while the plumber watches a live screen.
  4. 4Mark and note.Problem spots are noted by distance and, with a locator, marked on the ground.
  5. 5Review the findings.You see the images, hear what each finding means and get options for repair.

What to do before the plumber arrives.

Locate the clean-out.

It is often a capped pipe near the foundation or in the yard. Clear brush so the plumber can reach it.

Stop using water.

If the line is backing up, avoid flushing, laundry and dishwasher cycles until it is cleared.

List the symptoms.

Which drains are slow, when it started and whether sewage has surfaced.

Gather paperwork.

If you are buying, share the inspection timeline and any seller disclosures.

If sewage has backed into the house, keep children and pets out of the area and do not touch the water with bare hands. Wear gloves, and do not mix cleaning chemicals.

After the camera: what happens next.

Findings fall into three groups. Minor buildup or light roots mean cleaning. A crack, a sag or an offset may need a spot repair or a section replacement. A line that is failing along most of its length may call for full replacement.

Each path is covered on our sewer line repair page. We serve Estero and nearby areas, including Bonita Springs. Call with your findings, and dispatch can tell you what a visit would involve.
