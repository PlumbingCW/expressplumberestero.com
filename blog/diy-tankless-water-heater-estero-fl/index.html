Doing Your Own Tankless Water Heater Work in Estero: What Is Realistic

A tankless heater looks simple on the wall. The gas, venting and electrical behind it are not.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

The short answer for homeowners.

A homeowner can do some tankless maintenance, such as cleaning the inlet screen and flushing the unit with vinegar. A homeowner should not install a tankless heater or open its gas and venting components. Those jobs involve gas, combustion exhaust and high-capacity electrical work.

In Florida, plumbing work like this is generally done by a licensed contractor, and permits often apply. Rules vary, so ask the Village of Estero Community Development department before you start anything beyond basic upkeep.

Why tankless is harder than it looks.

A tank heater stores hot water. A tankless unit makes it on demand, so it has to heat a fast-moving stream in seconds. That takes a large burner or heating element, and the supply behind it has to match.

Gas models often need a larger gas line than the old tank did. Electric models can need a dedicated high-amperage circuit that your panel may not have room for. Gas units also need venting that carries exhaust outside, and a mistake there is a carbon monoxide risk.

Callers often tell us they bought a unit online and then found the existing gas pipe or vent was the wrong size. That discovery usually comes after the box is open, which is the expensive moment to learn it.

Jobs a homeowner can reasonably do.

Safe homeowner tasks on a tankless unit.

  • Read the display and write down any error code before you call anyone.
  • Clean the cold-water inlet screen if the manual shows how, after shutting off the water to the unit.
  • Run a descaling flush using the isolation valves, if your unit has them and the manual describes the steps.
  • Check that the area around the unit is clear of stored items and the vent opening is not blocked.
  • Set the temperature to the level the manufacturer recommends.

Read the manual before the first valve turn.

Every brand flushes a little differently. Some need a pump and hoses, some need the unit powered off first. Following the wrong order can damage the heat exchanger, so the manual for your exact model beats any general guide, including this one.

Descaling and flushing: what to expect in Estero.

Mineral scale builds inside the heat exchanger and slowly cuts flow and efficiency. Water in Estero comes from Lee County Utilities north of Williams Road and Bonita Springs Utilities south of it, according to a Village of Estero water workshop document. Check your bill to see which one serves you.

The sources we checked did not state a hardness number, so ask your utility or test your tap. The practical tie-back is simple: harder water means flushing more often. If your unit is throwing error codes or the hot water has gone weak, scale is a common suspect.

Flushing is a gentle job when done right. Skipping it for years and then forcing a flush on a heavily scaled unit can shake loose flakes that jam the valves. If the unit has never been flushed, a plumber can judge the condition first.

Permits and the Village of Estero.

The Village of Estero Community Development department takes building and trade permit applications, and a Trade Permit application exists on the Village website. Whether your tankless job needs one depends on what you are changing, so permit rules vary and the Village is the one to ask.

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition took effect on December 31, 2023, and permits applied for after that date follow it. An unpermitted gas or venting change can cause trouble with your insurer or at resale. A licensed plumber normally handles the permit paperwork for you.

What happens if you try the full install yourself.

  1. 1You size the unit.Flow rate and incoming water temperature set the size. Too small, and the shower goes cold when a second tap opens.
  2. 2You check gas or power.The old tank line is often too small. Upgrading it is licensed work and may need a permit.
  3. 3You handle the venting.Vent material, length and termination are set by the manufacturer and the code. A wrong vent can leak exhaust.
  4. 4You connect water and valves.Leaks at unions and isolation valves are common first-time mistakes.
  5. 5You test and start the unit.Gas pressure, combustion and error-free operation are checked with proper tools.

Most homeowners stop after step one, and that is the right place to stop. Our water heater installation page explains how a professional visit runs.

When to stop and call.

Call a licensed plumber right away if you see.

  • A gas smell. Leave the house, then call the gas utility from outside.
  • Water on the floor near the unit, or any water near electrical parts.
  • A flame color that looks yellow, soot around the unit, or a CO detector alarm.
  • Repeated error codes after a flush and a reset.
  • Hot water that swings between scalding and cold with no change at the tap.
