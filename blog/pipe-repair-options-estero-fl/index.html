Your Pipe Repair Options in Estero and How a Plumber Chooses Between Them

A leaking pipe can often be fixed without tearing out the whole system. The right repair depends on what the pipe is and why it failed.

By the Estero Express Plumbing team. Published October 24, 2026, updated October 24, 2026.

Call (239) 522-0887

The options at a glance.

Most pipe problems end up in one of four fixes: a spot repair on a small section, replacing a longer run, rerouting around the damage, or repiping the system. Which one fits depends on the pipe material, where it sits, how much of it is worn and why it failed.

A plumber cannot always tell from a description. Wet drywall tells us water is escaping, not which fix will hold. A pressure test, a meter check or a camera look usually settles it before any cutting starts.

Start by stopping the water.

  1. 1Shut the main valve.Turn off the water where the line enters the home. Everyone in the house should know where this is.
  2. 2Open a low faucet.Drain what is left in the lines and relieve pressure.
  3. 3Turn off the water heater power or gas.A heater without water can overheat or burn out its element.
  4. 4Move belongings and soak up water.Protect furniture and electronics, and photograph the damage for your insurer.
  5. 5Call a plumber.Say what you see, what you have shut off and where the wet spot is.

If water is near outlets, a light fixture or a panel, keep clear and switch off the breaker only if you can reach it dry. Water and electricity together are the bigger danger than the leak itself.

What the pipe is made of changes the repair.

Houses carry different materials in different decades. Many Estero homes date from the late 1990s through the mid 2000s, so the age of the plumbing is often twenty to thirty-five years. We do not assume a material for any community, because builders mixed products. Look at the pipe, or let a plumber identify it.

Copper can be repaired with soldered or press fittings, but pinhole leaks along a run suggest the wall is thinning everywhere. PEX, a flexible plastic, tolerates repair with approved fittings, though sunlight and chemicals damage it. Older rigid plastic and metal drain lines crack or corrode in their own ways.

One leak versus a pattern.

A single failure from a bump or a bad joint is a one-time repair. Pinholes in several spots, or a second leak a few months after the first, point to the pipe wearing out. Fixing each leak separately then costs more over time than addressing the run.

Pipes in walls, ceilings and under the slab.

Location can matter more than material. A leak in an attic or open wall cavity is a straightforward repair once someone reaches it. A leak in a line under the concrete floor is a different problem, since reaching it means breaking the slab or rerouting.

Warning signs of a slab line leak include a warm spot on the floor, a water meter that spins with every fixture off, and a bill that climbs for no clear reason. Our slab leak repair page covers how those are located and fixed.

Signs a pipe repair is needed.

  • A meter that keeps moving when every tap and appliance is off.
  • Stains or bubbling paint on a wall or ceiling.
  • The sound of running water when nothing is on.
  • Low pressure in one part of the house.
  • A musty smell or warped flooring with no obvious source.

When repiping makes more sense than another patch.

Repiping replaces the supply lines through the home with new pipe. It is a large job, so we do not suggest it for one isolated leak. It becomes worth discussing when leaks keep appearing, water comes out discolored, or access is easy enough to keep the job contained.

A plumber will walk the house, look at the pipe in the accessible spots and tell you plainly whether the rest looks sound. Ask for that honest read. See repiping for what the process involves.

What drives the price of a pipe repair.

Cost drivers, in plain terms.

Access.
An open attic run is quick. A wall that needs opening and patching adds labor and finishing work.
Length of pipe involved.
A short spot repair uses a fitting. A long run means more pipe and more joints.
Material.
Some materials and fittings cost more than others, and mixed systems need special transition fittings.
Water damage cleanup.
Drywall, insulation and flooring that got wet may need separate work.
Diagnosis time.
Finding a hidden leak with tools takes time before the repair itself begins.

Permits, code and checking your plumber.

Whether a pipe repair needs a permit depends on the scope. Small repairs are treated differently from replacing long runs. Permit rules vary, so ask the Village of Estero Community Development department, or Lee County if you live in an unincorporated area such as San Carlos Park.

Work on new permits follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition, which took effect on December 31, 2023. You can verify any plumber's licence at myfloridalicense.com before they open a wall.

Flush the taps after pipe work.

Lee County Utilities advises customers to flush taps for several minutes before drinking. That is good practice after any repair that opened a line, because debris and trapped air move through the first water.
