Test the water before buying a system.

The right system depends on what is in your tap water, and we do not guess. No hardness figure appears in the sources we checked, so we will not quote one. Test your tap with a home kit or a lab test, and ask your utility for its latest water quality report.

That one step saves money. A softener solves hardness. A carbon filter solves taste and chlorine odor. Neither fixes the other problem.

Warning signs your current system has a problem.

When a softener or filter needs attention.

  • Scale and spotting return even though the softener is running.
  • The brine tank has no salt use or is full of a hard crust.
  • Water pressure drops after the filter, which can mean a clogged cartridge.
  • Leaks or puddles around the unit or the drain line.
  • A strange taste or odor after a cartridge has gone unchanged.

Some callers say there is something wrong with their softener but cannot say what. That is fine. A plumber can check the valve, the brine tank, the drain and the bypass and tell you if the unit is worth repairing.
