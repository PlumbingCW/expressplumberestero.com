What we install for homes and small properties.

Installation covers anything new that connects to water, drain or gas lines. Most calls start with a plan, such as a bathroom remodel, a new kitchen island sink or a washer hookup in a spot that never had one.

Callers often ask whether we can install something they bought themselves. Usually yes, but we want the model number first. Some fixtures need extra fittings or a different drain size, and surprises on the day cost time.

  • Sinks, vanities, tubs, showers and their valves.
  • Toilets, bidets and plumbing for added half baths.
  • Garbage disposals, dishwasher hookups and ice maker lines. See garbage disposal service.
  • Washing machine boxes and laundry drains.
  • Outdoor hose bibs, utility sinks and irrigation tie-ins.
  • Water heaters, softeners and filters, each with its own page.

Before any work: what decides if it can be done.

Questions we answer at the site visit.

  • Is there a water supply close enough to tap into?
  • Can the new drain slope down to the existing line?
  • Will the fixture need venting, and where can the vent go?
  • Does the load fit the existing water pressure and pipe size?
  • Is the house on a slab, which limits where drains can be moved?

Drains need slope and a vent to work. That is why a sink in the middle of a room can cost more than one against a wall. A plumber can often find a workable route through the attic or a crawl space, but we cannot judge it by phone.

Permits, codes and who handles them.

Check permits before the walls open.

New plumbing is often permitted work. Permit rules vary, so ask the Village of Estero Community Development department, which accepts trade permit applications. If you are in San Carlos Park or another unincorporated area, ask Lee County instead.

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition took effect on December 31, 2023. Permits applied for after that date follow it. If you live in a gated community, also ask your association about its rules.

Source: Village of Estero website and local building department notice.

Pairing installs with other work.

A remodel is the cheapest time to fix old problems behind the wall. If supply lines are corroded, consider repiping while the wall is open. If you want cleaner water at the new sink, ask about a softener or filter.
