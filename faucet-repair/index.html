Emergency Faucet Repair in Estero, FL

A faucet that will not shut off or sprays under the sink is a water problem, not a nuisance. Call and we will sort repair from replacement.

Call (239) 522-0887

Where the water is coming from decides the fix.

Most faucet calls start with a symptom. A steady drip from the spout, water pooling around the handle, or a wet cabinet floor under the sink. Each points to a different part, so the first job is finding the exact spot.

Inside a typical faucet sit a cartridge or washers, rubber seals and a few small fittings. Those wear out with use, and mineral scale speeds the wear. A repair often means swapping one inexpensive part, not the whole faucet.

What the symptom usually points to.
What you see.Likely cause.Typical answer.
Drip from the spout when off.Worn cartridge, washer or seat.Replace the internal parts.
Water around the handle base.Worn O-rings or loose packing.Reseal or replace the cartridge.
Weak or uneven flow.Scale clogging the aerator or cartridge.Clean or replace the aerator and cartridge.
Wet cabinet under the sink.Supply line, shutoff valve or drain connection.Replace the leaking line or valve.
Handle spins loose or stiff.Worn stem or corroded cartridge.Replace the stem, or the faucet if parts are gone.

First steps while you wait for us.

What to do before the plumber arrives.

  • Turn the small oval handles under the sink clockwise to stop the supply to that faucet.
  • If those valves are stuck or leak, close the main shutoff for the house instead.
  • Set a bowl or towel under any drip and empty the cabinet so nothing soaks.
  • Do not force a frozen valve. A snapped valve is a bigger leak.
  • Photograph the faucet label or brand name if you can find it.

Repair or replace the faucet.

A fixture with one worn cartridge is a good repair candidate. A faucet that is pitted, corroded through or no longer sold with parts is usually cheaper to replace than to keep patching. We tell you which side yours is on after we see it.

Replacement is also the moment to check the shutoff valves and supply lines. Old braided lines and multi-turn valves fail quietly, and swapping them takes minutes when the sink is already open.

  • Kitchen faucets, including pull-down sprayers and side sprays.
  • Bathroom sink and tub or shower faucets, including diverter problems.
  • Laundry sink and utility faucets.
  • Outdoor hose bibs that drip or spray from the stem.

What changes the price of a faucet job.

Fixture type.
Tub and shower valves sit inside the wall, so they take longer than a sink faucet.
Condition of the valves.
A stuck or leaking shutoff under the sink adds a valve replacement.
Access.
A tight cabinet or a wall-mounted fixture slows the work.
Brand and parts.
Some brands keep parts in supply for years. Others do not.
