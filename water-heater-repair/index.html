Start with what the heater is doing.

A heater that makes no hot water at all is a different problem from one that makes lukewarm water. The first usually points to a lost power or gas supply, a failed element or a pilot or igniter problem. The second often means one failed element in an electric unit or sediment in the tank.

We cannot diagnose a unit from the phone, but the symptoms you describe help us bring the right parts. After that, a plumber tests the unit and tells you what failed.

Symptoms we see most often.

  • No hot water, or hot water that runs out within minutes.
  • Water that is warm but never gets hot.
  • A tripped breaker or a pilot that will not stay lit.
  • Rumbling or popping from the tank during heating.
  • Water dripping from the relief valve discharge pipe.
  • Rust-colored or smelly hot water.

Safety steps before anything else.

Gas smell or water near electric: stop first.

If you smell gas near a gas heater, leave the house, do not touch switches, and call the gas utility from outside. Then call us once you are safe.

If water is pooling around an electric heater, turn off its breaker only if the floor around the panel is dry. Otherwise stay out and call. Water and power do not mix.

Repair or replace: how we decide.

Parts like elements, thermostats, igniters, thermocouples and relief valves can be replaced on a tank that is otherwise sound. That is a repair, and it often makes sense on a unit that is not old.

A tank that leaks from its body cannot be patched. If your heater is also old, repeated repairs add up, and we will say so. See water heater replacement if that is where this is heading, or tankless service if you have an on-demand unit.

What changes the price of a repair.

Factors behind the number.

The failed part.
A thermostat or element costs less than a gas control valve. Parts are quoted before we replace them.
Gas or electric.
Each has its own parts and testing. Gas also involves venting and combustion checks.
Age and condition.
On an old tank, we weigh the repair against the life left in the unit.
Access.
Heaters in attics or tight closets take longer to work on than ones in a garage.
When you call.
Nights, weekends and holidays are emergency visits.
