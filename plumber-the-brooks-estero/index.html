What The Brooks is.

The Brooks is a gated, master planned development in Estero, off I-75. A local real estate profile puts it at roughly 2,400 acres, with more than half left as open space. Coconut Road runs along its southern edge and Three Oaks Parkway runs through the middle.

Local sources describe four gated communities inside it: Shadow Wood, Spring Run, Copperleaf and Lighthouse Bay. Boundaries between them are not published as a map we could find, so we describe them by name and by the roads around them.

Homes in their twenties.

If your Shadow Wood house went up between 1998 and 2005, it is now roughly 21 to 28 years old. Copperleaf villas finished in 2002 and 2003 are about 23 years old. That is not old for a house, but it is long enough that fixtures, supply lines and water heaters have had a full run of use.

We cannot tell you what is inside your walls from the phone, and we will not guess at pipe materials. What helps is a short check, done before something fails.

A twenty-minute check for a home of this age.

Water heater label.

Find the manufacture date. A tank heater commonly lasts about 8 to 12 years, so a unit from the original build or an early replacement may be due. See water heater replacement.

Shutoff valves.

Turn each fixture valve a quarter turn. Any valve that is stuck or weeping is worth replacing while the house is dry.

Toilet and sink supply lines.

Look for flexible lines that are bulging, rusty at the ends or stained. These cost little to swap and cause large messes when they split.

Water meter.

With every tap off, watch the dial for a minute. If it moves, call us about leak detection.

Condos, coach homes and shared plumbing.

Lighthouse Bay is mostly condos, coach homes and villas, and Shadow Wood and Copperleaf also include attached homes. In a building with a shared roof or shared walls, a leak in one unit can reach another. Who pays and who repairs depends on your association documents.

So the first call after shutting off your own water is often to the association or manager. Ask which pipes are yours and which are the building's. Then call us to find the source and tell you what needs repair.

  1. 1Close your unit's main.Find the shutoff for your unit and turn it clockwise. In a condo it may be inside a utility closet.
  2. 2Note where water is coming from.A stain on a ceiling can mean a leak above, not in your own pipes. Photograph it.
  3. 3Tell your manager.Shared lines are usually the association's concern. Put the report in writing.
  4. 4Call a plumber.Describe what you closed and what you saw. We will locate the source and explain the fix.
  5. 5Keep records.Photos and dates help with an insurance claim.

Why the mailing address can confuse a call.

The Brooks uses ZIP 34135, and some listings show a Bonita Springs mailing address even though local sources place the community in Estero. If dispatch asks for your street address instead of just the city, that is why.

For water, the Village of Estero says Bonita Springs Utilities serves properties south of Williams Road and Lee County Utilities serves properties north of it. Your bill names your provider, and that matters when a problem sits on the utility's side of the meter.

Sources: Village of Estero utilities page, Naples Condo Boutique and Homes.com profiles of The Brooks.

Drains, disposals and water that tastes off.

A drain that slows again a week after a plunger visit usually has buildup further down the line. A camera look decides between drain cleaning and a repair. In a multi-unit building, tell us if neighbors also have slow drains, since that points to the shared stack.

If your tap water has an odd taste or leaves spots, we cannot name a cause without a test, and we do not state a hardness figure for your street. Ask your utility for its water quality report. A softener or filter is one option once you know what you are treating.

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Village of Estero Community Development department whether a water heater swap or repipe needs one. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition took effect on December 31, 2023 and covers permits applied for after that date.
