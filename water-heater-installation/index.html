Installing on your schedule.

A planned installation gives you what an emergency does not: time. You can compare types, check the space, think about fuel and decide on a day that suits the household. Most of the cost surprises on this job come from decisions made in a hurry.

We install for homeowners upgrading, builders finishing a new house, and landlords updating a rental. The questions are the same in each case. What fuel is available, where will the heater sit and how much hot water does the house use?

Choosing the type of heater.

Water heater types for a new install.
Type.Fits best when.Install considerations.
Gas tank.A gas line is already in the house.Needs proper venting and a gas connection.
Electric tank.No gas, or a simple like-for-like swap.Needs the right breaker and wire size.
Tankless.You want on-demand hot water and have the supply.Needs gas or electrical capacity and venting. See tankless service.
Heat pump tank.There is open space with airflow.Needs clearance, a drain for condensate and power.

We confirm what your plumbing, venting and power supply allow before you commit. A phone call cannot answer that, and we say so.

What a good installation includes.

Parts of the job that matter.

  • A new shutoff valve on the cold supply so you can isolate the heater.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe run to a safe spot.
  • A drain pan with a drain line where the heater sits above living space.
  • An expansion tank where the plumbing needs one.
  • Correct venting for gas units and the right wire and breaker for electric ones.
  • A temperature set at a safe level, and a walk-through of the controls.

How installation day goes.

  1. 1Call and describe the house.Tell dispatch the fuel type, the room, the size of the household and any existing heater.
  2. 2On-site check.The plumber looks at the space, supply lines, venting and the electrical panel or gas line.
  3. 3Options and price.We explain the choices and give a clear price before work starts.
  4. 4Install.We shut off water and power or gas, set the unit, connect water, fuel and venting, and fill the tank.
  5. 5Test and review.We check for leaks and proper operation, set the temperature, and show you the shutoff and relief valve.

What affects the installation price.

Factors that shape the price.

Type of unit.
Tank, tankless and heat pump models differ in equipment cost and in work required.
Capacity.
Households with more people and fixtures need a larger tank or higher flow.
Changing fuel or location.
Moving a heater or switching between gas and electric adds piping, venting or electrical work.
Code updates.
A new install may need a pan, relief piping, an expansion tank or venting brought current.
Access.
Attics and tight closets take longer than a garage or utility room.

Permits, code and your Estero address.

Check the rules before the heater arrives.

New water heater installs are commonly permitted. Rules vary, so ask the Village of Estero Community Development department whether your job needs a permit. If your home is in unincorporated Lee County, ask Lee County instead.

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition took effect on December 31, 2023, and permits applied for after that date follow it. Confirm any plumber's licence at myfloridalicense.com before you hire.

Source: Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition notice, and DBPR licence lookup.

Estero homes and heater sizing.

Many Estero houses went up between the late 1990s and the mid 2000s, and their original heaters often sit in a garage or an interior closet. Garage installs are usually simpler to reach. Interior closets need more care with venting and a drain pan.

Water supply matters too. Bonita Springs Utilities serves south of Williams Road and Lee County Utilities north of it, according to a Village of Estero water workshop document. Check your bill, then ask your utility about water hardness, since it affects how long a heater lasts.
