Sewer Camera Inspection in Estero, FL

Repeated backups, a home purchase or a mystery smell all call for a look inside the pipe. Call to schedule a camera visit.

Call (239) 522-0887

Why look inside the pipe at all.

A sewer line is buried, so guessing is expensive. You can pay for repeated cleanings, or dig the wrong spot, or replace a pipe that only needed cleaning. A video inspection replaces the guess with a picture of the pipe.

The camera head is mounted on a flexible cable with a light. We feed it through the cleanout and watch a monitor as it travels toward the street connection. Anything unusual in the pipe shows on screen.

Good reasons to schedule an inspection.

  • Backups or slow drains that return after cleaning.
  • You are buying a home and want to know the condition of the line.
  • Large trees close to the house or the path of the pipe.
  • A damp or sunken area in the yard with no clear source.
  • Plans for a renovation that will add fixtures to the line.

What the camera can and cannot tell us.

What a sewer camera shows.
Finding.What it looks like.What it usually means.
Root intrusion.Fine or thick fibers entering at joints.Roots found a gap. Cleaning cuts them back, repair seals the gap.
Crack or break.A visible split in the pipe wall.May need repair, lining or replacement depending on length.
Belly or sag.A low spot where water pools.Debris collects there and clogs recur. Often needs correction.
Offset joint.Pipe sections that no longer line up.Shifting soil. Catches debris and may let roots in.
Grease or scale buildup.Rough, coated walls.Cleaning or jetting may restore the pipe.

A camera cannot see through a fully blocked line, so we may clear the blockage first. It also cannot give an exact depth without a locator, and it does not test for leaks that only open under pressure. We will tell you plainly what the picture shows and what it does not.

How the inspection goes.

  1. 1You call.Dispatch asks about symptoms and whether there is a cleanout. We schedule a visit.
  2. 2We locate the access point.A cleanout outside is ideal. Otherwise we may pull a toilet or use another entry.

  4. 4Camera run.We feed the camera down the line and watch for defects while it travels.

Buying or selling in Estero.

Older neighborhoods and the camera.

Many Estero houses date from the late 1990s through the mid 2000s, which places their sewer lines in a range where a baseline inspection makes sense, especially before a purchase. Their age alone does not predict a problem, which is exactly why a camera view beats an assumption.

Who owns which part of the line varies by address. Ask your utility where your private line stops, and ask the Village of Estero Community Development department if findings lead to a repair that needs a permit.
