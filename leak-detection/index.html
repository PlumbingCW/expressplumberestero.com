How to tell you have a hidden leak.

Most people call us because of a clue, not because they saw water. The bill is higher with no change in use. The meter keeps turning when everything is off. A floor feels warm, or a wall stains. Those are all reasons to look.

Some leaks are loud and some are silent. A pipe in a slab or an underground line can leak for weeks before anything shows at the surface. Finding it early limits the damage.

Clues that point to a leak.

  • A water bill creeping up with no change in how you use water.
  • A meter that moves with every fixture and appliance off.
  • A warm spot on a tile or slab floor.
  • Stained or bubbling paint, a soft drywall patch or a musty smell.
  • Wet ground or low pressure with no obvious cause outdoors.
  • The sound of running water when the house is quiet.

A two-minute check you can do yourself.

  1. 1Turn everything off.Close every tap, stop the dishwasher and washer, and make sure no one is flushing.
  2. 2Read the meter.Find the meter box, lift the lid and look for a spinning dial or a leak indicator. Note the reading.
  3. 3Wait and look again.Check after fifteen minutes. If the number has moved, water is going somewhere.
  4. 4Check toilets.A drop of food coloring in the tank shows a silent toilet leak if it reaches the bowl without flushing.
  5. 5Shut off the main if you must.If water is visibly escaping, close the main valve and call us.

How a plumber actually finds the leak.

Finding a leak is a process of elimination. A pressure test on the supply lines shows whether the leak is on the house side. Acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized pipe. A thermal camera can show temperature changes where hot lines leak behind a wall.

Equipment narrows the search, but a person decides what the readings mean. We cannot name the spot from the phone, and we will tell you if a first pass does not settle it. If the leak turns out to be under a concrete floor, see slab leak repair.

Leak risks around Estero.

Slabs, age and a vacant house.

Estero sits at about 16 feet of elevation in Lee County, and many Florida homes are built on concrete slabs. A leak under a slab is hard to see, so a warm floor spot or a rising bill deserves a call.

Many local owners are away for part of the year. If you leave a house empty, close the main water valve and ask someone to check the meter while you are gone. A leak in an empty home can run for weeks.
