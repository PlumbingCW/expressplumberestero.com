How a tankless heater differs.

A tankless unit heats water only when a tap is open. There is no storage tank to leak, and the hot water does not run out as long as the unit keeps up with demand. The trade-off is that the unit needs a lot of fuel or power at the moment you use it.

That is why installation details matter. A gas unit needs a large enough gas line and proper venting. An electric unit needs significant electrical capacity. We check both before suggesting one.

Why flushing matters.

Minerals in water leave scale inside the heat exchanger. Scale reduces flow, causes error codes and makes the unit work harder. A flush clears it out. Manufacturers generally recommend periodic flushing, so check your manual for the schedule.

Ask your utility about the hardness of your water, because it changes how often a flush is needed. The sources we checked did not give a figure for Estero, so we do not guess. If your unit has never been flushed and is several years old, a flush is a sensible first step.

Tankless in Estero homes.

Utility water and permits.

Estero homes get water from Bonita Springs Utilities south of Williams Road and Lee County Utilities north of it, according to a Village of Estero water workshop document. Both treat water in ways that affect scale. Ask your utility about hardness, and flush on the schedule your unit's maker recommends.

Tankless installs often involve gas, venting or electrical changes, which are commonly permitted. Rules vary, so ask the Village of Estero Community Development department. Under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, 8th Edition, which took effect December 31, 2023, a licensed plumber handles the details.
