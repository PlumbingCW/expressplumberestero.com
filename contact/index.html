What to tell dispatch.

Information that speeds things up.

Your address and ZIP.

ZIPs like 33928, 34135 or 33912 help us place you. Street address confirms it.

What you see or hear.

Dripping, spraying, gurgling, a hum from a disposal, a heater that will not light.

What you have shut off.

Tell us if the main, a fixture valve or the heater power is already off.

The age and type of equipment.

For water heaters, tank or tankless, gas or electric, and the age if known.

Who can give access.

Tenants, condo managers or neighbors. Say who will be there and who approves the work.

Before the plumber arrives.

Safe steps while you wait.

  • Close the main water valve if water is spreading.
  • Turn off power to a flooded water heater or appliance at the breaker, only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility from outside.
  • Do not pour drain chemicals into a backed-up line, and never mix them.
  • Take photos of damage for your insurer before cleanup.
  • Move valuables and rugs away from wet areas.

Calling about a rental or business.

Landlords, property managers and business owners call us often. Give dispatch the address, the contact at the site, and who authorizes the spend. We explain the price before any work, so you can approve it by phone.

For shops and offices, mention whether water can be shut off to part of the building. Our commercial plumbing page covers the kinds of calls we take.

Not sure which service you need.

You do not need the right word. Describe the symptom. A meter that keeps moving, a wet ceiling or a bill that creeps up often points to leak detection. Sewage in a tub points to sewer line cleaning. No hot water points to water heater repair.
