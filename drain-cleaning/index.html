One slow drain or the whole house.

The first thing we want to know is how many drains are affected. One sink that drains slowly usually has a clog in its own trap or branch line. A tub, a toilet and a floor drain that all misbehave together point to a blockage further down, in the main line.

That difference decides the tools, the time and the price. It is also the reason we ask questions on the phone before we send anyone out.

Signs a drain needs professional cleaning.

  • Water that drains slowly, then stops, a few days after you plunged it.
  • Gurgling from a drain when a different fixture runs.
  • A bad smell near a floor drain, tub or laundry standpipe.
  • Water backing up into a tub or shower when you flush a toilet or run the washer.
  • The same clog returning within weeks of being cleared.

What to do before we arrive.

  1. 1Stop adding water.Do not run the dishwasher, washer or showers. Every gallon has to go somewhere, and a blocked line sends it back up.
  2. 2Skip the chemical cleaner.Never mix drain products. A strong cleaner sitting in a pipe is a splash hazard for the plumber and can damage older fittings.
  3. 3Try a plunger once.A cup plunger on a sink or a flange plunger on a toilet can clear a soft clog. If it does not move after a few tries, stop.
  4. 4Protect the floor.Put towels around a backing-up drain and keep children and pets away from standing water.
  5. 5Call and describe it.Tell dispatch which drains are slow, what went down them last, and whether water is coming up anywhere.

How we clear a clogged drain.

A plumber starts with the simplest tool that will work. A hand auger or a cable machine breaks up or hooks the clog in a sink, tub or branch line. A main line with roots or heavy buildup may need a larger cable machine with a cutting head.

When a cable only punches a hole through the blockage, the clog tends to come back. If grease or scale coats the pipe wall, we explain whether hydro jetting would do a better job. If a line keeps clogging for no clear reason, a camera look at the pipe tells us what is really there.

What affects the price of drain cleaning.

Factors that move the price.

Which drain.
A sink trap is quick work. A main line takes more time and heavier equipment.
Access.
A cleanout outside the house makes the job simpler. With no cleanout, the plumber may have to go in through a toilet or roof vent.
Cause of the clog.
Hair and soap clear easily. Roots, scale or a collapsed section need more than a cable.
Tools used.
Hand tools, cable machines, jetting and a camera each carry a different charge.
Time of day.
Nights, weekends and holidays are emergency calls and are priced as such.

Drains in Estero homes.

Older homes, shared lines and seasonal use.

A good share of Estero homes were built between the late 1990s and the mid 2000s, and Shadow Wood alone has 1,481 homes from 1998 to 2005. Drain lines in houses that age have had two or three decades of use, so slow-draining kitchen and laundry lines are common calls.

Many homes here sit empty for part of the year. A drain that has not been used for months can dry out its trap, which lets sewer smell into the house. Run every drain for a minute when you return, and call us if the smell stays. Sewer service varies by area, so ask your utility who maintains the main line past your property.

Keeping drains clear between visits.

  • Wipe grease from pans into the trash, not the sink.
  • Use a drain strainer in every shower and tub.
  • Flush only toilet paper. Wipes sold as flushable still catch in pipes.
  • Run hot water for a minute after washing dishes.
  • Never pour paint, concrete slurry or coffee grounds down a drain.
